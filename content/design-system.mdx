## Charts

A chart is a figure that carries axes. It owns the grid, the tooltip and the
legend, and takes its colours from the five chart tokens, so every chart on the
site reads the same and follows the accent without being told.

## A decorated chart

A chart accepts children, and they are drawn **inside the plot**. They read the
scales the chart already built, through Recharts' own hooks, so a decoration
lines up with the curve above it by construction and keeps doing so when the
width changes.

`SpectrumBand` is the whole idea in sixty lines: it asks the x axis where 400nm
and 700nm land, asks the y axis where 20% sits, and paints a gradient between
them. Reading both scales rather than counting pixels is what keeps it in place
when the domain changes.

A chart may also carry **a series computed from the others**, recomputed as the
legend is used. `Result` is the visible channels summed and clamped, it wears the
colour that sum would actually be, and it appears only from two channels on:
below that it lies exactly on the one curve it combines, which reads as a
rendering fault rather than as a result.

A series can also name its own colour, which is the right call exactly once: when
the colour is the subject. Here they are **the pure sRGB primaries**, because the
result is their additive sum and summing anything else gives a wash rather than
white. The data are measured points interpolated with Catmull-Rom, not three
formulas fitted to look right: curves tuned one at a time all saturate together,
and their sum becomes a flat line that says nothing.

## Confusion matrix

A heatmap, which is the form this data is read in everywhere it appears, and
still a real `table` underneath: the counts stay selectable and a screen reader
reads each with its row and column. The cells are square and spaced rather than
flush, a continuous field reading as an image where separated tiles read as
counts. Hover one: every row means *of all the Xs, how many were called Y*, and
the grid alone makes a reader count along two axes to recover that. The ramp is
`--heat-from` to `--heat-to`, a pair that exists because no other token can
stand in: it has to run from the page toward a far end that stays legible
under inverted text, and **the direction of that run flips with the theme**.
